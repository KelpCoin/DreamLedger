Clear sources. Clear limits.

Evidence matters more than confident wording.

DreamLedger aims to help people compare options and check claims without hiding missing information. We distinguish what a page says from what a source supports, and what a checkout offers from what has actually been purchased and delivered.

Before we describe something as verified

1. Find the sourceUse the original document, official terms, direct statement, or other suitable evidence.
2. Check the contextRecord the date, version, market, scope, and important exclusions.
3. Look for conflictsKeep missing details, counterexamples, and contradictory evidence visible.
4. Confirm the outcomeA listing is not a sale. A checkout is not settlement. A purchase is not proof of delivery.

What we will not imply

  • That an offer has customers just because a page exists.
  • That a product or service is available when its purchase path has not been confirmed.
  • That a company-reported result is an independent finding.
  • That a missing price, fee, or condition is zero or free.
  • That we hold certifications, customer results, or uptime guarantees without evidence.

How to read a claim

Supported by a source means the cited material supports the wording, not that every detail has been independently proven. Unverified means the evidence is not sufficient. Contradicted means reliable evidence conflicts with the claim. Out of date means the information may no longer be current.

For important decisions, open the original source and check the current terms. Our tools are aids to judgment, not professional legal, financial, medical, or procurement advice.

Security and reporting

Never share passwords, API keys, payment card details, or private customer information in a public report. If you find a public-page problem, include the affected URL, what you expected, and what happened instead.
