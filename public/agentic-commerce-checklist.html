Agentic commerce checkout readiness checklist

A purchase journey is only as reliable as its weakest handoff. Use this checklist to inspect the path from discovery to delivery, with evidence for each step. A green-looking dashboard is not a substitute for a completed end-to-end test.

How to use it

For every item, record PASS, FAIL, or UNKNOWN, attach a timestamped artifact where practical, and name the person or system responsible. Do not mark an item PASS from a design document alone.

1. Discovery and offer clarity

The offer describes exactly what is included, excluded, priced, and delivered.Evidence: public offer page, price, terms, delivery format and expected timing.
An automated buyer can discover the offer and retrieve the current details.Evidence: canonical URL, machine-readable metadata, product identifier and a fresh retrieval test.
Availability is truthful.Evidence: checkout-enabled state matches actual capacity and fulfillment readiness.

2. Authority and buyer intent

The buyer or agent has authority for the specific purchase.Evidence: explicit authorization record, scope, amount limit, identity or delegation context as appropriate.
The action stays within a stated spending and tool budget.Evidence: enforced cap at each worker level, denial test above the cap, and revocation test.
A human approval gate exists for consequential or irreversible actions.Evidence: approval request, approver, decision, timestamp and exact action approved.

3. Checkout and settlement

Checkout resolves to the intended live product and amount.Evidence: a fresh test in the appropriate mode and verified product/price identity.
The server verifies the provider's signed event using the unmodified request body.Evidence: signature-valid test, invalid-signature rejection, and raw-body handling.
Duplicate or delayed events cannot create duplicate entitlements.Evidence: idempotency test with replayed and out-of-order events.
The ledger distinguishes checkout creation from settled, attributable payment.Evidence: provider payment identifier, live-mode status, amount, currency, product attribution, settlement state and reconciliation result.

4. Fulfillment and evidence

Fulfillment starts only after the required payment state is verified.Evidence: order-to-payment linkage and a negative test for unpaid or unmatched sessions.
The delivered item or service is identifiable and retrievable.Evidence: scoped entitlement, delivery timestamp, artifact hash or receipt, and buyer-facing retrieval path.
Failure, cancellation, refund and support paths are documented and tested.Evidence: test cases, owner, expected response and reconciliation treatment.

5. Final acceptance

Do not call the journey complete until an independent test can discover the offer, authorize the action, complete checkout, verify settlement, receive the promised fulfillment, and inspect the evidence. A signed receipt can prove integrity of a record; it does not by itself prove the underlying claim is true or still current.

This checklist is general operational guidance, not legal certification, security certification, or a guarantee of sales or protocol eligibility.
