What to compare

1. Equivalent scope

Specifications, quantities, inclusions, exclusions, standards, warranty and service level. Do not compare unlike products as if they were identical.

2. Total landed cost

Unit price, setup fees, packaging, freight, insurance, duties, taxes, currency conversion and any recurring charges.

3. Delivery risk

Lead time, dispatch date, delivery location, stock status, dependencies and what happens if the supplier is late.

4. Commercial terms

Deposit, payment milestones, credit terms, quote validity, cancellation, refunds, warranty and dispute process.

5. Evidence quality

Source document, quote date, named supplier, traceable contact route and whether each claim is documented or merely asserted.

6. Open questions

List every material unknown and ask suppliers the same questions so the answers are comparable.

A defensible comparison workflow

  1. Collect the original quotes and record their dates and currencies.
  2. Normalize quantities, units and scope without changing the supplier's original terms.
  3. Calculate comparable totals only where the inputs are supported; label estimates separately.
  4. Flag missing, contradictory or expired information.
  5. Ask the same unresolved questions of each supplier.
  6. Choose against your priorities, and keep the source evidence with the decision.

Example: why headline price is not enough

Supplier A quotes 100 units at $8 each with freight excluded. Supplier B quotes 100 units at $8.60 each including freight. Supplier A looks cheaper at first glance, but its landed cost is unknown until freight and any other charges are confirmed. A sound comparison does not invent that missing number.

Who this is for

Small businesses, procurement teams, operators, importers, contractors and anyone comparing supplier or vendor offers across different currencies, quantities or delivery terms. The method works internationally; always confirm local tax, customs and regulatory obligations for your transaction.
