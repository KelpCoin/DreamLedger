What is in scope

  • One public URL or one bounded digital artifact accessible without bypassing authentication.
  • Up to five acceptance criteria supplied by the customer.
  • One report. Any additional work must be separately agreed.

Not included: penetration testing, legal or regulatory certification, exhaustive security review, private account access, production changes, or a guarantee that a product is safe, correct, compliant, or commercially successful.

Example acceptance criterion

Criterion: The deliverable documents every API endpoint in the agreed scope.

Pass evidence: An accessible source file or published page lists each endpoint and method, and matches the agreed scope.

Fail evidence: The list is absent, incomplete against the agreed scope, or contradicts the artifact.

Needs human review: The relevant source is inaccessible or the criterion is too ambiguous to evaluate consistently.

Before a review can begin

  1. The customer supplies the artifact reference and acceptance criteria.
  2. Accessibility and scope are checked. Missing information remains a blocker, not an assumed pass.
  3. The reviewer records what was actually checked and the available evidence.
  4. The report records limitations and any criteria not assessed.
