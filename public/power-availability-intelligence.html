AI power is becoming a procurement constraint.

On 6 October 2026, Google and Constellation announced a 20-year agreement intended to add 890 MW of new nuclear capacity to the PJM grid through upgrades at 11 existing units. The announcement sits inside a broader power-demand shift driven by data-center development.

SOURCE-BOUND OBSERVATION

890 MW of additional reliable nuclear capacity is the concrete commitment in the announced Google–Constellation agreement. EIA separately forecasts record U.S. electricity consumption in 2026 and 2027 and identifies data-center development as a major driver.

The economic event

Large computing loads are no longer only a real-estate or compute procurement problem. Reliable power availability, interconnection timing, generation commitments and regional grid conditions can become gating variables for project economics. The public evidence does not establish a specific buyer's willingness to pay, nor does it prove that any individual project will face a shortage. It does establish a commercially important signal: major technology buyers are signing long-duration power arrangements while U.S. electricity demand is reaching record levels.
