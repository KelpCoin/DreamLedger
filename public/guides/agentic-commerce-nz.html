What does an AI shopping agent need to buy a physical product?

The difficult part is not making an agent click a checkout button. The difficult part is giving it enough trustworthy information and authority to act without turning commerce into guesswork.

1. A real product

An agent needs an identifiable item, current availability, condition where relevant, price, currency and seller. “Something like this” is not inventory.

2. Honest terms

Shipping, returns, taxes, delivery constraints and any buyer requirements need to be visible enough for the agent to compare the offer honestly.

3. Bounded authorization

The buyer needs control over what the agent may do. A useful purchase authority has a product or category boundary, spending limit, and clear expiry or revocation behaviour.

4. A legitimate checkout

The agent needs a route from an accepted offer to the merchant's actual payment system. A link is only the beginning. Payment has to settle externally.

5. Fulfilment evidence

For physical goods, payment alone does not prove delivery. The system needs evidence that the seller fulfilled the agreed item and that the buyer's order can be reconciled to it.

6. Independent verification

The strongest economic record is not the agent saying “done.” It is an independent process recomputing the relevant claim from retained records and external evidence.

The practical test: if you cannot distinguish a proposed purchase, an authorized purchase, a settled payment and a fulfilled order, the system is not ready to call itself autonomous commerce.

Why New Zealand matters

New Zealand has already seen authenticated agentic transactions on Mastercard's network. The direction is real, but merchant-side readiness still depends on accurate machine-readable product information and trustworthy transaction controls.
