Australian Red Cross Society

ABN 50 169 561 394 · sample prepared 27 September 2026 · primary financial period FY2025

Registration

Australian Red Cross Society is incorporated by Royal Charter and is registered with the Australian Charities and Not-for-profits Commission (ACNC). Its 2024-25 annual report identifies the Society as ABN 50 169 561 394 and states that it comprises Australian Red Cross Lifeblood and Australian Red Cross Humanitarian Services.

The ACNC explains that its public Charity Register contains registration, program, people, financial and compliance information, including whether reporting is up to date and whether the regulator has taken action.

Sources: ACNC Charity Register · Australian Red Cross Annual Report 2024-25

Responsible persons

The current Australian Red Cross governance page identifies Kate Jenkins AO as President, Claire Rogers as Board Chair and Penny Harrison as Interim CEO. The governance reforms that separated the President and Board Chair roles became effective on 1 July 2025.

The 2024-25 annual report records the transition from Charles Burkitt as Board Chair and identifies the governance reform as a significant change during the reporting period.

Sources: Australian Red Cross Governance · Executive team · FY2025 Annual Report

Financial summary

  • Total revenue: A$1.11077 billion.
  • Total government funding: A$935.303 million.
  • Donations, bequests and partnerships: A$76.517 million.
  • Total assets: A$969.976 million.
  • Total liabilities: A$637.630 million.
  • Net assets: A$332.346 million.
  • Cash and cash equivalents: A$199.612 million.
  • Net deficit for FY2025: A$40.450 million, compared with A$51.346 million in FY2024.

Government funding represented approximately 84.2% of FY2025 revenue. Total lease liabilities were A$291.088 million, including A$258.217 million non-current.

The independent auditor reported that the financial report gave a true and fair view and complied with the applicable ACNC Act, Australian Accounting Standards and ACFID reporting requirements.

Source: Australian Red Cross Annual Report 2024-25, financial statements and auditor report

Red flags / diligence points

Consecutive annual deficits. FY2025 reported a A$40.45m deficit after a A$51.35m deficit in FY2024. This warrants examination of the causes and sustainability of the operating position.
Funding concentration. Government funding was A$935.3m of A$1.1108b revenue. This is a material funding concentration that merits understanding by anyone assessing financial resilience.
Governance transition. The President and Board Chair roles were separated from 1 July 2025, with new appointments during the transition period. This is a monitoring point rather than evidence of misconduct.
Related-party disclosure. The FY2025 report states that a member of Alex Mathieson's family was employed in the Deputy CEO's team. The report discloses the relationship and states remuneration was at arm's length.
Lease obligations. Total lease liabilities were A$291.1m at 30 June 2025. The report also records A$35.224m of principal lease payments during FY2025.
These are diligence flags derived from disclosed public records. They are not allegations of wrongdoing and should not be treated as such.

External benchmark

GiveRadar's published Integrity Assessment lists Australian Red Cross Society at 34/100 and describes the result as “Limited data available”. GiveRadar reports a peer average of 52/100 for Australian human-services charities. Its published explanation says the low score reflects limited public data, not concerns about the organisation.

This benchmark is included for context only. It is a third-party scoring methodology, not an ACNC finding and not evidence of misconduct. A paid report should reconcile such external scores against primary-source evidence rather than treating the score itself as a conclusion.

Source: GiveRadar Integrity Assessment

Risk rating

MODERATE · screening assessment

The sample rating reflects the combination of consecutive reported deficits, high government-funding concentration, significant lease liabilities and a major governance transition. Countervailing evidence includes substantial assets and cash, positive operating cash flow, continuing operations and an unmodified independent audit opinion.

This rating is a structured screening conclusion, not an audit opinion, fraud determination, credit rating or recommendation to donate, invest or contract.
