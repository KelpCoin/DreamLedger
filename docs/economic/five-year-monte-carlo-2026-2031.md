# Five-Year Monte Carlo: 2026 -> 2031

## Purpose

Illustrative scenario analysis of the current DreamLedger trajectory. This is not a forecast and not a claim about future wealth.

## Current starting point

- Verified external revenue: NZ$0
- Independent external buyers: 0
- Verified economic outcomes: 0
- Existing substrate: research, extraction, comparison, document generation, evidence packaging, fulfillment, Stripe, CUBE/Cortex/Gauntlet and human gates
- Immediate commercial experiment: real buyer → small paid offer → fulfillment → independent proof
- Major constraint: external buyer acquisition, not another architecture layer

## Monte Carlo model

100,000 simulated paths over 60 months.

Three uncertain transitions were modelled:
1. First independent payment.
2. Repeatable commercial offer.
3. Scalable revenue engine.

Base-case illustrative assumptions:
- 2.5% monthly chance of reaching a first payment while still pre-revenue.
- 6% monthly chance of reaching repeatability after the first payment.
- 1.5% monthly chance of reaching a scalable stage after repeatability.
- Revenue growth after repeatability: approximately 8% monthly, volatile.
- Revenue growth after scalability: approximately 12% monthly, volatile.
- 35% of simulated revenue is treated as retained wealth after fulfillment, operating costs and leakage.

These probabilities are assumptions for stress-testing the trajectory, not measured probabilities.

## Base-case simulated five-year distribution

Approximate retained wealth after 60 months:

- 10th percentile: NZ$0
- 25th percentile: NZ$132
- 50th percentile: NZ$1,821
- 75th percentile: NZ$11,657
- 90th percentile: NZ$56,188
- 95th percentile: NZ$151,816
- 99th percentile: NZ$937,208

Probability of exceeding:
- NZ$10,000: 26.8%
- NZ$100,000: 6.8%
- NZ$1,000,000: 0.9%

These figures are model outputs, not predictions.

## Interpretation

The simulation says the current trajectory is highly path-dependent.

If the system remains mostly in infrastructure/build mode and does not repeatedly cross the external buyer gate, five-year retained wealth remains near zero to low five figures in most simulated paths.

The distribution changes sharply after three events:
REAL BUYER → REPEATABLE OFFER → SCALABLE ACQUISITION.

That means the highest-leverage variable is not adding more internal machinery. It is increasing the rate at which the existing substrate encounters real buyers and converts one of them into a settled, fulfilled, independently provable transaction.

## Amber Room test

The simulation does not establish that the Amber Room is likely or unlikely.

It establishes the structural requirement:

NZ$0 → first independent payment → repeatability → scalable acquisition → compounding retained cash/equity.

The first payment is therefore the calibration event. Once a real transaction exists, the model can be rebuilt from observed conversion, price, fulfillment cost, repeat rate and acquisition data rather than assumed probabilities.
