Take-or-Pay Offtake Commitment

Reusable component: TAKE-OR-PAY-COMMITMENT

State transition: NAMED BUYER → BINDING OFFTAKE → MINIMUM PURCHASE VOLUME → CONTRACTED TERM → PRODUCTION → DELIVERY / PAYMENT → EVIDENCE

Observed economic event

On 31 August 2026, Smackover Lithium announced its second commercial offtake agreement for the South West Arkansas Project, this one with LG Energy Solution. The binding take-or-pay agreement covers 8,000 metric tonnes per year of battery-quality lithium carbonate for 10 years after commercial production begins. The company estimated the agreement at US$1.5 billion over the 10-year period.

Why this is a new 777 primitive

This is not capacity reservation. Capacity reservation monetizes priority access to scarce future production capacity. A take-or-pay commitment instead creates a contractual purchase floor: the buyer commits to an economic volume whether or not every unit is ultimately taken, subject to the contract terms.

The reusable observation is therefore the conversion of uncertain future production into a named buyer-backed minimum demand stream. It can be detected across commodities, manufacturing, energy, chemicals, data infrastructure, logistics and other markets where future output is contracted before full production.

Detection fields

  • Named buyer / counterparty
  • Binding agreement status
  • Minimum annual or periodic volume
  • Contract term
  • Take-or-pay or deficiency-payment mechanism
  • Start condition, such as commercial production
  • Published economic value, if available
  • Underlying asset or production stream
  • Primary-source evidence
