# Customer Prepayment Commitment: Boost Run / Cohere Date: 2026-10-07 ## Primitive A customer prepayment is a commercial structure in which the buyer pays part of a contracted future service commitment before the provider has delivered the contracted capacity or service. This is materially different from a capacity reservation. A reservation can secure priority access or manufacturing capacity without the same cash conversion. A prepayment converts part of future contracted demand into cash earlier, while the buyer typically retains delivery, acceptance, refund, or termination protections. ## Source-bound event Boost Run and Cohere executed a service agreement effective 30 September 2026 for dedicated GPU cloud infrastructure and related services. Boost Run's SEC-filed agreement records Cohere as the customer. The disclosed commitment is approximately US$525.6 million over the agreement term, with a portion payable as a prepayment. Initial infrastructure acceptance is expected in Q2 2027. The agreement also creates a critical evidence boundary: if a specified minimum amount of infrastructure has not been accepted by 15 July 2027, Cohere may terminate and receive a refund of prepaid amounts. The commercial state is therefore not simply "large contract signed". It is a state machine linking advance cash to future acceptance. ## Reusable state machine NAMED BUYER → SIGNED SERVICE AGREEMENT → PREPAYMENT → DELIVERY / ACCEPTANCE → SERVICE COMMENCEMENT → EARNED REVENUE → FULFILLMENT EVIDENCE Failure branch: PREPAYMENT → ACCEPTANCE THRESHOLD MISSED → TERMINATION RIGHT → REFUND OF UNAPPLIED PREPAYMENT ## Why this is a new component This is not take-or-pay. Take-or-pay establishes a minimum purchase obligation over a future term. This is not capacity reservation. Capacity reservation secures future supply access. This is not forward-flow capital. Forward-flow monetizes a portfolio of receivables or contracted cash flows through a financing structure. Customer prepayment is the distinct primitive: cash moves forward in time from buyer to supplier against a future delivery obligation, with acceptance and refund logic defining the boundary between cash received and revenue earned. ## Commercial extraction Reusable offer hypothesis: **Prepayment Structure Brief**.
