Five checks before trusting a commercial claim

Use this before acting on a price, performance promise, supplier statement, product claim, or business opportunity. The goal is not to distrust everything. It is to make the evidence strong enough for the decision.

1. Find the original source

Prefer a source document, official terms, product page, filing, measurement, or direct statement over a repost. Keep the URL or document reference and exact passage supporting the claim.

2. Check the date and scope

Record when the information was published and which version, market, location, customer group, or period it covers. A once-accurate claim can become stale.

3. Separate fact from interpretation

Write down what the source literally says. Label estimates, opinions, forecasts, and inferences separately. Do not upgrade “may”, “up to”, or “typical” into a guarantee.

4. Look for disconfirming evidence

Search for exclusions, corrections, counterexamples, conflicting numbers, independent reviews, and conditions hidden in the small print. Record contradictions rather than ignoring them.

5. Match the evidence to the decision

A claim can be true but irrelevant. Ask whether it answers the actual question, whether important evidence is missing, and what happens if the claim is wrong.

Use cautious labels

Supported means the cited source supports the wording, not that every part is independently proven. Unverified means evidence is insufficient. Contradicted means reliable evidence conflicts with the claim. Stale means it may no longer be current. Unknown means available information does not justify a conclusion.
