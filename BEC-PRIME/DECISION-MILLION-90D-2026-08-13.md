# 90-Day Million-Dollar Decision Map

Goal: maximize probability of NZD 1,000,000 gross revenue in 90 days using existing BEC-PRIME/DreamLedger infrastructure, local-first automation, horizontal catalogue, seller/buyer tunnels, and approval-gated commerce.

## Ranked pivots

1. Productized AI revenue recovery for SMBs
Sell one narrow outcome: recover dormant leads/quotes/customers from existing CRM/email exports. Price $2,500-$10,000 setup. Fulfillment is mostly automated and evidence is measurable.

2. B2B automation pods marketplace
Do not build a generic marketplace first. Seed 3-5 outcome-specific pods and sell them as fixed-scope implementations through the horizontal catalogue. Examples: lead reactivation, quote follow-up, inbox triage, catalogue enrichment, customer-support triage.

3. AI commerce readiness / agentic-commerce implementation
Package the existing structured catalogue, checkout, approval, webhook and fulfillment machinery as an implementation for merchants preparing for agentic buying. Stripe documents agentic commerce as a current private-preview direction, including structured product data and humanless checkout flows.

4. Revenue-recovery audit as a diagnostic funnel, not the business
If a low-ticket audit is used, it must exist only as a paid diagnostic that leads to a much larger implementation. Never let the diagnostic become the public identity of the company.

5. Marketplace-in-a-box for niche operators
Clone the horizontal catalogue + account + approval + payment + evidence stack into a deployable package for one niche at a time. Charge implementation plus transaction or success fee only where contractually appropriate.

6. Lead-intent radar as a managed service
Use the existing signal-ingestion, scoring and hardening machinery to identify public buying signals for a specific vertical, then deliver a daily qualified target queue. Human approval remains required before outreach.

7. B2B catalogue migration / enrichment factory
Sell conversion of messy supplier/product spreadsheets, PDFs and legacy catalogues into structured commerce-ready product data. This fits the existing ingestion, normalization, SKU and projection machinery.

8. Agent-ready product feed service
Provide merchants with structured product feeds, machine-readable metadata, checkout routing and evidence hooks designed for AI-agent discovery. Monetize setup and recurring maintenance only after demand is proven.

9. Acquisition of distressed micro-commerce assets
Use the system as an operating layer for small under-monetized catalogues, domains or niche stores. Buy cheaply only when cash flow and transferability can be verified. Apply the horizontal catalogue and automated fulfilment stack to improve conversion.

10. License the operating system/IP after proving one repeatable loop
Do not sell the architecture story first. First produce one repeatable paid outcome, then package the proven workflow as a license, implementation kit or managed deployment.

## The million-dollar math

Do not target one million through $29-$49 transactions. The target requires high-value contracts or a large number of transactions.

Examples:
- 100 customers x $10,000 = $1,000,000
- 40 customers x $25,000 = $1,000,000
- 20 customers x $50,000 = $1,000,000
- 10 customers x $100,000 = $1,000,000

The most credible path is therefore a productized B2B implementation with a narrow outcome, followed by cloning once payment and delivery are proven.

## 90-day operating rule

Week 1: choose ONE offer and one buyer class. Make one functional catalogue card and one tunnel. No architecture expansion.

Weeks 1-2: manually close the first customer. Capture receipt, delivery proof and ledger evidence.

Weeks 2-4: repeat the same offer until there are 3-5 paid examples. Remove anything that requires custom work repeatedly.

Days 30-60: clone the proven delivery loop into adjacent targets. Add approved automation only where the evidence shows a bottleneck.

Days 60-90: pursue larger contracts, channel partners and licensing while the proven delivery engine keeps operating.

## Non-negotiables

- Public surface is horizontal catalogue first.
- CTA cards enter focused tunnels.
- Auction slots remain placeholders until auction functionality is actually ready.
- No fabricated inventory, customers, revenue or testimonials.
- Every public action remains approval-gated.
- MTG and adult silos remain isolated.
- No Rabbit Mode until a repeatable paid loop has real evidence.
- Phone-first operator workflow: approvals, exceptions and revenue evidence must be manageable from a phone.
