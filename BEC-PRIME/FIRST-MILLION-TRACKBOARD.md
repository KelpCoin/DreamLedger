## On-track criteria

$0 -> $1:
- EBAY-001 passes.
- A real person requests the service.
- A real payment settles.
- The requested result is delivered.

$1 -> $1K:
- At least 5 paid transactions.
- No fabricated revenue.
- Fulfilment time is measured.
- Refunds and failures are recorded.
- The same offer can be sold repeatedly.

$1K -> $10K:
- At least one acquisition channel repeatedly produces qualified prospects.
- Human fulfilment time is falling.
- Contribution margin remains positive.
- The best demand cluster is promoted ahead of weaker ideas.

$10K -> $100K:
- The loop is automated where evidence supports automation.
- Marketplace coverage expands only when useful.
- Repeat/referral behaviour is measured.
- Distribution is larger than the founder's direct outreach capacity.

$100K -> $1M:
- Multiple validated verticals.
- Reliable acquisition.
- Reliable fulfilment.
- Strong unit economics.
- Operational monitoring.
- Capital is reinvested into the highest-return bottlenecks.

## Million-dollar math

The target is not one magical product.

Examples:
- 10,000 customers x NZ$100 contribution = NZ$1,000,000.
- 4,000 customers x NZ$250 contribution = NZ$1,000,000.
- 1,000 customers x NZ$1,000 contribution = NZ$1,000,000.

The actual mix is determined by observed demand, pricing power, fulfilment cost, acquisition cost, repeat rate, and referral rate.
