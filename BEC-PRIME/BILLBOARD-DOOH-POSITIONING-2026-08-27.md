# Internet Billboard: DOOH-Inspired Positioning

Date: 2026-08-27
Experiment: INTERNET-BILLBOARD-2026-08-27

## Strategic decision

The Internet Billboard is not physical DOOH inventory. It is a web-native permanent media artefact that borrows useful DOOH creative principles without making a false claim about physical reach or impressions.

The commercial object is scarce digital real estate on a finite 1,000,000-pixel canvas. The standard 100x100 Founding Tile is NZ$50. Placement is promised until January 1, 3000, subject to the site continuing to operate. Image placement is a NZ$100 add-on and requires human approval.

## What the DOOH research contributes

IAB's formal DOOH definition describes digital out-of-home as digital screens used for marketing outside the home. Its core features include dynamic content, data integration, placement variety, contextual relevance and optional programmatic buying.

The 2026 IAB New Zealand market barometer reports that DOOH is now a significant part of the media mix for 68 percent of surveyed industry professionals. Static creative remains dominant at 92 percent usage, while contextual or triggered creative is used by 60 percent and video by 52 percent. Buyers also continue to want clearer measurement and value proof.

The Internet Billboard therefore adopts the following principles:

1. Make the creative visually immediate.
2. Keep the message simple enough to understand at a glance.
3. Make the destination obvious and clickable.
4. Treat context and audience as part of creative quality.
5. Make the inventory finite and inspectable.
6. Never promise impressions that have not been measured.
7. Keep publication behind a human brand-safety gate.
8. Make the permanent year-3000 story the distinctive memory device.

## The positioning

Old positioning:

Rent a tile for 30 days.

New positioning:

BUY A PIECE.
LEAVE IT UNTIL 3000.

Supporting line:

One million pixels. Multiple footprints. One permanent canvas.

The standard entry point is NZ$50 for 100x100 pixels. Larger footprints consume more of the same finite Founding pool. Once the available space is gone, it is gone.

## Why the price stays high

NZ$50 is deliberately not framed as cheap banner inventory. It is the entry price for a permanent, finite internet artefact. The buyer is purchasing participation in a public landmark, not a promise of cheap traffic.

The product must therefore look expensive before it is expensive. The landing page uses strong typography, visual hierarchy, scarce inventory language, an inspectable canvas and a premium review gate.

## What is deliberately excluded

No fake sales.
No fabricated impressions.
No fake countdown.
No guaranteed traffic.
No crypto requirement.
No resale marketplace.
No prize draw.
No automatic publication of user images.

These may only be added after economic evidence justifies them.

## Evidence boundary

The research supports the creative and media-positioning principles above. It does not prove that a buyer will pay NZ$50 for this specific Internet Billboard. That remains an empirical question and is resolved only by settled payments.
