SHOULD I STILL GO? - DECISION PACK

Use this when you have already agreed to an event and are now wondering whether you should actually attend.

DECISION GRID
Score each question 0, 1, or 2.

1. Do I genuinely want to go?
2. Would going leave me better off overall?
3. Is the main reason I want to cancel a temporary feeling that may pass?
4. Is there a concrete cost, risk, or boundary problem with attending?
5. If nobody were disappointed, what would I choose?
6. Have I already committed in a way that makes cancelling materially costly?
7. Can I attend while keeping reasonable boundaries?
8. Will staying home solve the actual problem?
9. Am I going mainly to avoid somebody else's reaction?
10. Which choice will I be more comfortable owning tomorrow?

0 = strongly against going
1 = mixed / uncertain
2 = strongly in favour of going

THREE-WAY RESULT

GO
The balance points toward attending. Make the plan smaller and simpler if that helps: decide when you will arrive, how long you will stay, and what boundary you will keep.

DON'T GO
The balance points toward not attending. Be truthful and brief. You do not need a courtroom-grade argument for a personal boundary.

PAUSE
The evidence is genuinely mixed. Set a short deadline for deciding, then stop re-litigating the decision until that deadline.

FAST TEST
Imagine the event is cancelled by somebody else. Do you feel relief or disappointment? Notice the first reaction, then check it against the concrete facts.

IF YOU GO
Decide your arrival time, exit time, transport plan, spending limit, and one boundary you will keep.

IF YOU DON'T GO
Send a short truthful message. Avoid inventing elaborate excuses that create another problem later.

IF YOU NEED MORE TIME
Give yourself a specific decision deadline. Do not leave the other person waiting indefinitely.

DO NOT SEND
- A message written mainly to provoke reassurance.
- A long justification you do not actually believe.
- A promise you cannot keep.
- A hostile message because you feel cornered.

ONE-SENTENCE RULE
Choose the option you can honestly live with after the immediate emotion has settled.

FINAL CHECK
Am I making this decision because it is what I want or need, or because I am trying to control somebody else's reaction?

This is a general reflection aid, not professional mental-health, medical, legal, or emergency advice.
